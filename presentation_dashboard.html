🎯 Executive Summary & Objectives

This presentation synthesizes the empirical findings, model evaluations, and statistical diagnostics across two standard Data Science regression labs:

  • Automobile Price Prediction: 201 vehicles, analyzing how engine size, curb weight, horsepower, and fuel efficiency determine market price.
  • Laptop Price Prediction: 238 laptops, investigating processor clock speed, RAM, SSD capacity, core count, and GPU on retail pricing.
  • Methodological Rigor: Moving beyond naive in-sample $R^2$ to rigorous 80/20 train/test splits, 5-fold cross-validation, and Gauss-Markov diagnostic testing.
  • Software Reliability: Executed end-to-end with zero warnings (replacing deprecated Seaborn functions) and verified via automated assertion test suites.
Key Strategic Insight: Simpler, well-regularized models (MLR and low-degree polynomials) consistently outperform high-degree polynomial expansions on unseen test data.

⚠️ The Overfitting Trap: Degree 1 vs 3 vs 11

Runge's Phenomenon: Degree 11 polynomial wiggles aggressively at boundary points (40-55 mpg). While training error is minimized, test error surges ($RMSE = $7,370).

📉 Laptop Dimensionality Collapse (Poly Deg 2)

Curse of Dimensionality: Expanding 7 features into degree 2 created 35 terms. With only 190 training laptops, the model memorized noise, producing a negative Test R² (-0.05).

🔍 Multicollinearity Diagnostics (VIF)

In automobiles, engine-size and curb-weight exhibit VIF > 5.5 and pairwise correlation $r = 0.85$. This multicollinearity masks the independent effect of fuel economy.

💡 Key Recommendations for Production

  • Always Validate Out-of-Sample: In-sample $R^2$ is an unconstrained optimizer of training error and cannot be trusted alone.
  • Enforce Scikit-Learn Pipelines: Encapsulating StandardScaler within a pipeline eliminates data leakage during cross-validation.
  • Audit Feature Multicollinearity: Run VIF tests before drawing causal inferences about individual coefficient signs.
