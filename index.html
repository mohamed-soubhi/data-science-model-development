🎯 Executive Summary: The 4-Domain Data Science Journey

This master presentation synthesizes empirical findings across three real-world domain studies:

  • Automobile Pricing Study: Uncovering continuous correlations, detecting extreme class imbalance (only 3 rear engines), and isolating engine size as the dominant driver ($F = 67.95, p < 10^-22$).
  • Laptop Pricing & Model Refinement: Diagnosing the collapse of high-degree polynomials (degrees 4 and 5 drop to negative R²), and using GridSearchCV to tune Ridge regularization ($lpha=100$).
  • Healthcare Insurance Cost Capstone: Demonstrating how large sample sizes ($n=2,772$) enable polynomial interaction terms (`smoker * bmi`) to excel without overfitting (Test $R^2 = 0.834$).
  • King County Housing Capstone: Predicting residential home prices ($n=21,613$) from square footage to multi-feature standardized polynomial pipelines ($R^2 = 0.751$) and regularized Ridge regression.
The Golden Rule of Model Complexity: Polynomial interaction terms destroy generalization on small samples ($n < 200$), but unlock superior predictive power at enterprise scale ($n > 2,000$).

💡 Key Recommendations for Production

  • Sample Size Dictates Complexity: Use MLR on small datasets ($n < 300$); reserve Polynomial Pipelines for larger datasets ($n > 2,000$).
  • Always Regularize Expanded Feature Spaces: Tune Ridge $lpha$ via GridSearchCV to control variance.
  • Standardize Before Expanding: Encapsulate StandardScaler in a Scikit-Learn Pipeline to prevent data leakage.
